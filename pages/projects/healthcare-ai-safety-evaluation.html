Conversational AI Safety Evaluation

During my AI consultancy placement at Ortom, I contributed to the evaluation of a conversational AI platform developed for Support Never Stops, a drug harm-reduction and wellbeing service. My work focused on assessing the assistant's safety, response reliability, policy compliance and consistency with its curated knowledge base through structured Python tests, Pydantic Evals and LLM-as-a-Judge techniques.

My focus: Evaluation design, automated testing, multi-turn scenarios, knowledge-base grounding, safety assessment and documentation. Ortom designed and delivered the wider platform as a team.

01 / The Challenge

Support Never Stops serves people who use drugs, those concerned about someone else's drug use, and professionals working in the field. The platform aims to provide factual, region-specific information and support through a conversational interface.

In this sensitive context, general-purpose AI systems present particular risks, including hallucinated information, fabricated references and inappropriate responses to high-risk situations.

Another challenge was ensuring that the assistant's answers remained grounded in its curated knowledge base, which included reference documents provided by the client. Without appropriate grounding, an LLM could generate a plausible answer that was not supported by the available information.

My evaluation challenge was to translate these reliability and safety requirements into repeatable tests that could assess the assistant's behaviour across different conversation scenarios.

02 / My Approach

I contributed to a structured evaluation framework designed to simulate realistic conversations and compare the assistant's responses against defined expected outcomes. The evaluation combined deterministic assertions with LLM-based assessment where response quality required more contextual judgement.

  • Scenario design: Developed structured test scenarios covering routine interactions, unusual inputs, multi-turn conversations and safety-related edge cases.
  • Expected outcomes: Defined the behaviours that each scenario should demonstrate, including appropriate responses, safety boundaries and escalation where necessary.
  • Automated execution: Used Pydantic Evals to execute structured test scenarios and capture evaluation results consistently.
  • Programmatic assertions: Applied checks to verify expected response characteristics, required information and conversational workflow behaviour.
  • Knowledge-base grounding: Evaluated whether factual responses were supported by the curated library of client-provided reference documents, checking for unsupported claims, invented information and hallucinations.
  • LLM-as-a-Judge: Used an LLM-based evaluator to assess responses against defined criteria when simple exact-match checks were insufficient.
  • Review and documentation: Reviewed evaluation outcomes and documented findings to support assessment and refinement of the conversational assistant.

03 / What I Evaluated

The evaluation covered different aspects of conversational safety, reliability and factual grounding. The categories below are high-level descriptions rather than reproductions of confidential test cases.

Evaluation Area What It Checks
Conversation Flow Whether interactions follow expected steps and respond appropriately to user input.
Multi-turn Consistency Whether the assistant maintains appropriate behaviour across a sequence of messages.
Safety-critical Responses Whether high-risk situations receive appropriate safety-oriented handling and escalation.
Policy Compliance Whether responses respect defined boundaries around unsupported or disallowed advice.
Knowledge-base Grounding Whether factual claims are supported by the curated reference documents rather than invented information or unsupported sources.
Privacy & Confidentiality Whether the assistant avoids inappropriate disclosure of user information.

What the Evaluation Demonstrated

The framework provided a repeatable way to test defined behaviours and examine responses beyond surface-level fluency. Some requirements could be checked programmatically, while others required a contextual assessment of the assistant's response.

An important aspect of the evaluation was assessing factual grounding. A response could sound convincing but still contain claims that were absent from or inconsistent with the available knowledge base. Checking alignment with the provided reference material therefore formed part of assessing response reliability.

Interpreting evaluation results: Passing a predefined evaluation suite provides evidence that the assistant met the requirements of the scenarios tested. It does not guarantee factual correctness or safe behaviour across every possible real-world conversation.

05 / Reflection & Limitations

This work strengthened my understanding of LLM evaluation engineering, automated testing and responsible AI. One of my main takeaways was that evaluating conversational AI requires more than checking whether a response is fluent or relevant. It is also necessary to assess whether the response is safe, consistent with defined requirements and grounded in reliable source information.

The experience also highlighted the importance of carefully designed evaluation criteria. Deterministic checks provide consistency for well-defined requirements, while LLM-based evaluators can assess more nuanced responses but may introduce their own uncertainty.

Scenario-based testing cannot cover every possible conversation or guarantee that responses will always remain grounded in the knowledge base. For a sensitive-use assistant, automated evaluation should be supported by continuous testing, additional scenario coverage, human review and appropriate oversight.
